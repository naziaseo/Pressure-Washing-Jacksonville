Why Jacksonville Dryer Vents Get Blocked

Your Dryer Vent Works Hard in Jacksonville Humidity

A dryer vent has a simple job: let warm, damp air out of your home. In Jacksonville, that job is harder than it looks. The humidity that hangs in the air, the dust and pollen that settle on every outside surface, and the lint every load of laundry produces all meet at one small vent cover — and every load adds another layer.

That is why drying gets slower so gradually that most people do not notice until a load takes two cycles. The vent flap starts to stick, the cover gathers dust and cobwebs, and the air that should be flowing out freely has to push through lint at the exit. A quick wipe of the cover helps, but the lint packed at the exit and the flap that will not open properly need a proper clean and an airflow check.

Good to know: when you request a quote, tell us where the outside vent is and whether your laundry room is on an outside wall. This service covers the exterior vent only — if the duct inside the home is blocked, we will tell you, and you may need a specialist. Older, worn vent covers and deep lint inside the duct may only improve, not look new.

Four Signs to Look For

How to Tell Your Dryer Vent Needs Cleaning

Run a normal load and watch the dryer. If any of these sound familiar, your vent is telling you something.

01

Clothes Take Longer to Dry

Clothes taking longer to dry is the most common sign. Reduced airflow can make a load take much longer than it used to.

02

The Dryer Feels Very Hot

The dryer feeling very hot, or the laundry room feeling warm and damp, can mean the warm air is not getting out.

03

Visible Lint at the Vent

Visible lint at the outside vent, or dust and cobwebs around the cover, means lint is building up at the exit.

04

The Vent Flap Will Not Move

Lint can stop the vent flap from opening and closing properly. If the flap stays shut when the dryer runs, air is not flowing out.

Gentle on Vents

Why High Pressure Is Wrong for Dryer Vents

The Controlled Way

The vent cover and the area around it are cleaned with a controlled, low-pressure method. Lint at the exit is removed by hand where it can be reached, then the airflow is checked with the dryer running — so the vent stays intact and the result is checked, not guessed.

What High Pressure Can Do

High pressure can damage the vent cover, bend the flap, and push lint and water further into the vent instead of out of it. That is why we do not use high pressure on dryer vents at all.

  • Checked first, then cleaned
  • Low pressure on the vent, always
  • Vent cover, flap and airflow checked
  • Rinsed and inspected at the end

Which method is right for your vent? There is no single method for every vent. The right approach depends on the vent type, the condition and the type of buildup. More pressure does not automatically mean better cleaning — on a vent, it usually means a damaged cover or a flap that will not close.

Pro Tip

Check the Flap, Not Just the Cover

After the dryer has been running for a few minutes, step outside and look at the vent flap. It should be open while the dryer runs and closed when it stops. If it stays shut while the dryer is on, air is not getting out — even if the cover looks clean from the yard. Check it after a storm too: leaves and debris settle around outside vents first. If the flap moves freely through the next few weeks, the vent is usually clear.

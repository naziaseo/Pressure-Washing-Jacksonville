Florida vs Your Fence

Florida Has a Fence Problem

Florida sun, humidity, sprinklers and shade hit every fence and deck material differently. Wood turns grey and green, vinyl goes green with algae, and decks collect leaves and pollen in every corner.

Different fence and deck materials react differently to pressure and cleaning treatments. That is why the material and condition are considered first — before any cleaning method is chosen.

Grey Is Not Always Gone

A Grey Fence Makes the Whole Yard Look Tired

A grey, weathered fence can make the whole yard look neglected — even when the house is clean. But grey wood is usually only the surface layer. It is the top of the wood that has weathered, not the whole board.

Sprinklers, shade and humidity feed the greying and the green growth on top of it. Cleaning removes the visible buildup so you can see what the wood really looks like underneath.

01

Sun

Florida sun bleaches wood grey and fades the colour out of it over time.

02

Sprinklers

Sprinklers that hit the fence keep it wet — and wet wood grows algae.

03

Shade

Shaded fences stay damp longer, so mildew and green growth settle in.

04

Humidity

Jacksonville humidity keeps outdoor wood damp, and damp wood greys faster.

Myth or Fact

Fence Facts

Three things people say about fences. Tap a board to see if it is true.

Myth

Grey is usually only the surface layer. Cleaning can bring the wood back. Damaged or rotten boards are a different story — they need repair, not washing.

Fact

Stain will not bond to wet or dirty wood. Let the surface dry properly and be inspected first, then coat it.

Myth

High pressure tears the grain and leaves wood fuzzy and splintered. A controlled, lower-pressure clean is the right way to treat wood.

Planning to stain or seal your fence or deck? Clean it first. Stain will not bond to dirty or mouldy wood, so a wash before coating helps the finish look better and last longer. Let the surface dry properly and be inspected first.

Before We Arrive

What to Do Before Fence & Deck Cleaning

A few small steps before we arrive help the wash go smoothly.

  • Move chairs, tables, grills, planters, and other furniture away from the cleaning area.
  • Remove decorations, toys, tools, outdoor equipment, and other loose items.
  • Keep nearby doors and windows closed while cleaning is underway.
  • Move or protect items that may be sensitive to water or cleaning treatments.
  • Mention damage, loose boards, delicate landscaping, painted areas, staining, or other conditions needing consideration.

Fence & Deck Cleaning FAQs

Common Questions

Can you clean a wood fence?

Suitable wood fences can be professionally cleaned using a controlled method based on wood condition and type of buildup.

Can you clean a vinyl fence?

Yes, suitable vinyl fencing can be cleaned to address dirt, grime, algae, mildew, and outdoor residue.

Can pressure washing damage a wood deck?

Improper pressure or technique can potentially damage wood. The deck condition should be assessed before choosing the method.

Can you clean composite decking?

Suitable composite decking can be cleaned, but manufacturer care recommendations and material condition should be considered.

Will fence cleaning remove algae and mildew?

Professional cleaning can address visible algae and mildew on suitable fence materials. Results vary with the amount and condition of buildup.

Can cleaning prepare my deck for staining?

Cleaning can be part of preparation for staining on suitable wood surfaces. The deck should dry properly and be inspected before coating.

How often should a fence or deck be cleaned?

There is no universal schedule. Weather, shade, moisture, vegetation, material, and visible buildup can affect timing.

Will cleaning remove every stain?

No cleaning method can guarantee removal of every stain. Results depend on the type, age, depth, and condition of the stain.

Do I need to be home during fence or deck cleaning?

Not necessarily, depending on property and access arrangements. Access requirements can be discussed before service begins.

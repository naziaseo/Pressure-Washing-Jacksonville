Short Answer

Yes, It Can — But the Risk Is Small

Yes, it can. The cleaning solutions used in pressure washing and soft washing can burn grass and damage plants if they sit on leaves or pool on the lawn. But with the right preparation, careful rinsing, and runoff control, the risk is small. Here is what causes the damage — and what we do to prevent it.

Why Damage Happens

Why Pressure Washing Can Harm Grass

Most professional house washing and roof cleaning uses a soft wash method: low pressure plus a cleaning solution, usually sodium hypochlorite (liquid bleach) mixed with water and a soap that helps it cling to surfaces. That mix is very good at killing mold, mildew, and algae on your siding and roof. The problem is that the same mix does not know the difference between algae on your house and the grass next to it.

01

Overspray

A breeze carries fine mist onto nearby plants and flower beds.

02

Runoff

Solution runs off the roof or siding and collects on the lawn below, where it sits instead of draining away.

03

Drying on Leaves

Solution left on foliage in hot sun burns leaves faster than solution that is rinsed off.

04

Too Strong a Mix

A stronger mix than the job needs raises the risk for everything it touches.

Florida lawns are especially exposed, because most Jacksonville homes have grass, shrubs, and flower beds right up against the house — exactly where the wash water lands.

Our Protection Routine

How We Protect Your Lawn and Plants

Plant protection is part of the job, not an extra. Here is what we do on every house wash and roof soft wash.

01

We Pre-Wet the Lawn and Plants

Before any cleaning solution goes on the house, nearby grass, shrubs, and beds get a thorough rinse with plain water. Wet leaves absorb far less solution than dry ones, and wet soil dilutes anything that reaches the ground.

02

We Cover Delicate Plants

Prized flowers, new plantings, and anything fragile get covered before we start. If you have a plant you are worried about, point it out and we will cover it.

03

We Control Runoff

We work in sections and keep wash water moving toward drains and driveways instead of letting it pool on the lawn.

04

We Rinse During and After

Plants near the work area are rinsed again while we work and once more when the job is done, so no solution is left sitting on foliage.

05

We Use the Right Mix for the Job

The cleaning solution is matched to the surface and the buildup — no stronger than it needs to be.

06

We Watch the Heat

On very hot, sunny days, we take extra care with timing and rinsing, because solution dries faster and burns more easily in direct sun.

Before We Arrive

What You Can Do Before We Arrive

A little preparation from you makes protection even better.

  • Water the lawn the morning of the job, or the evening before. Damp soil and wet leaves are your lawn’s first line of defense.
  • Move potted plants away from the house, especially small pots near walls being washed.
  • Point out prized plants. Tell us about anything you care about most — a favorite rose bush, new sod, a vegetable bed — and we will give it extra attention.
  • Cover anything very delicate if you prefer, and let us know about ponds or water features near the work area.

Before You Hire Anyone

Questions to Ask Any Pressure Washing Company

Before you hire anyone — us or a competitor — ask these four questions. The answers tell you a lot.

01

Do you pre-wet plants and lawn before you start?

Pre-wetting is the first line of defense — wet leaves absorb far less solution than dry ones.

02

How do you handle runoff so it does not pool on my grass?

Runoff that pools on the lawn sits and burns. It should be kept moving toward drains and driveways.

03

What do you do if a plant is damaged?

A company with a real routine will tell you plainly — not change the subject.

04

Will you rinse plants again after the job is done?

A final rinse makes sure no solution is left sitting on foliage.

A company with a real plant-protection routine will answer all four without hesitating.

The Bottom Line

Pressure Washing Does Not Have to Hurt Your Lawn

Pressure washing does not have to hurt your lawn. The risk is real, but it is manageable — pre-wet, cover, control runoff, rinse, and use the right mix. That is how we protect your grass on every job.

Lawn Protection FAQs

Common Questions

Can pressure washing kill my grass?

Yes, it can — cleaning solutions used in pressure washing and soft washing can burn grass and plants if they sit on foliage or pool on the lawn. Pre-wetting plants, covering delicate ones, controlling runoff, and rinsing during and after the job keeps the risk small.

Is soft washing safer for plants than pressure washing?

Soft washing uses lower pressure, which is gentler on surfaces, but it still uses a cleaning solution — so plant protection matters either way. The method matters less than the preparation: pre-wet, cover, control runoff, rinse.

What should I do if a plant looks stressed after cleaning?

Rinse the plant thoroughly with plain water and give it a few days — most mild leaf burn recovers with watering. If you have concerns after our visit, call us at 877-367-5979 and tell us what you see.

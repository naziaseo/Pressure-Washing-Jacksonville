Short Answer

Yes — With a Hose, Soap and a Soft Brush

Yes, you can clean a pool deck yourself: rinse it with a garden hose, wash it with mild soap and water, and scrub with a soft brush or broom. What you should not do is blast it with high pressure, pour straight bleach on it, or let dirty water run onto your lawn. Here is how to do it safely — and when it is time to call a pro.

Why Pool Decks Go Green

Why Pool Decks Go Green and Black in Florida

A pool deck in Jacksonville sits wet, shaded and warm for much of the year. That is exactly what algae and mildew need — and it is why a deck that looked clean in spring can be slippery and dark by late summer.

01

Humidity & Shade

Warm, humid air and shade from trees and screen enclosures keep a deck damp long after rain.

02

Algae & Mildew

Green and black growth feeds on moisture and shade. It spreads fast on concrete, pavers and cool deck.

03

Leaves & Pollen

Leaves, pollen and dirt settle on the deck and stain it, especially in spring and after storms.

04

Standing Water

Water that pools on a deck instead of draining away leaves stains and speeds up growth.

A green or black pool deck is not just ugly — it is slippery. Algae on a wet deck is a slip risk, especially for children and older family members.

Do It Yourself

What You Can Safely Do Yourself

Light cleaning is safe for most homeowners. Work on a cool, cloudy day if you can, and keep the steps below in order.

01

Rinse First

Sweep leaves and dirt off the deck, then rinse the whole surface with a garden hose. Loose dirt comes off easily when wet.

02

Use Mild Soap Only

Wash with mild dish soap mixed in a bucket of water. Avoid harsh chemicals and never pour straight bleach on the deck.

03

Scrub With a Soft Brush

Use a soft-bristle brush or broom on the surface. A wire brush can scratch concrete and damage sealed pavers.

04

Work in Small Sections

Clean one section at a time and rinse as you go, so soapy water does not dry on the surface and leave marks.

05

Rinse the Plants Nearby

Rinse grass and flower beds next to the deck with plain water before and after, so no soapy water sits on foliage.

06

Let It Dry Fully

Let the deck dry before anyone walks on it barefoot, and check for slippery spots that need a second pass.

Avoid Damage

What NOT to Do on a Pool Deck

A few common mistakes damage pool decks — and the plants around them.

  • Do not use high pressure on cool deck or pavers. It can damage the surface coating, loosen joint sand and etch concrete.
  • Do not pour straight bleach on the deck. It can burn plants, stain surfaces and harm pool water if it runs in.
  • Do not let dirty wash water run into the pool. Rinse toward drains and away from the water.
  • Do not scrub sealed pavers with a wire brush. It scratches the sealer and the paver surface underneath.
  • Do not clean in the hottest part of the day. Soap and solution dry fast in direct sun and leave marks.

When to Call a Pro

When a Pool Deck Needs a Professional

Light cleaning is a good DIY job. Call a pro when the deck is past what a hose and brush can fix.

01

Green or Black That Won’t Rinse Off

If algae and mildew stay after soap and scrubbing, the deck needs a proper cleaning treatment.

02

Sealed Pavers or Cool Deck

Sealed pavers and cool deck coatings need the right method — the wrong pressure damages them.

03

A Large or Slippery Deck

Big decks, steep slopes and very slippery surfaces are safer and faster with professional equipment.

04

An HOA Deadline

The Bottom Line

A Clean Pool Deck Without the Risk

A pool deck does not need high pressure to get clean. Mild soap, a soft brush and a good rinse handle most of it — and what they cannot handle is a job for a pro with the right method. Either way, keep the wash water off your plants and out of your pool.

Pool Deck FAQs

Common Questions

Can I pressure wash my pool deck myself?

You can rinse and wash it with mild soap and a soft brush. High pressure is risky on cool deck and sealed pavers — it can damage the surface. If the deck is very dirty or slippery, a professional cleaning is the safer choice.

Will cleaning hurt my pool water?

It can, if dirty wash water runs into the pool. Keep rinse water moving toward drains and away from the pool, and never pour bleach or cleaning solution where it can run in.

How often should a pool deck be cleaned?

There is no fixed schedule. Humidity, shade, trees overhead and how often the pool is used all affect how fast algae and dirt build up. Clean it when it starts to look green or feel slippery.

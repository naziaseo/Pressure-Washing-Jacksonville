PROFESSIONAL PAVER CARE

Professional Paver Cleaning & Sealing in Jacksonville

Pavers are exposed to rain, humidity, sunlight, dirt, pollen, leaves, foot traffic, and everyday outdoor conditions. Over time, these factors can leave paver surfaces looking dull, discolored, or affected by algae, mildew, weeds, and organic buildup.

Our paver cleaning and sealing service in Jacksonville, FL focuses on cleaning suitable paver surfaces and addressing visible buildup across the pavers and their joints. The surface condition, joint material, and overall condition of the pavers are considered before selecting an approach.

After cleaning and proper preparation, paver sealing may be considered when suitable. Sealing is a separate treatment from cleaning and depends on the condition and type of paver surface.

Pavers and Joints Need Careful Attention

Cleaning should be performed with the paver material, joint condition, and existing buildup in mind. Excessive pressure can disturb joint sand, while damaged or loose pavers require identification before cleaning.

VISIBLE BUILDUP

Common Paver Problems We Address

Results vary with the type, age, depth, and condition of buildup or staining. Structural and installation problems are separate from cleaning.

Dirt & Grime

Regular outdoor exposure can leave pavers covered with dirt and grime. Cleaning can help remove visible surface buildup.

Algae & Mildew

Moisture can contribute to algae and mildew growth on some paver surfaces.

Weeds in Joints

Weeds, grass, leaves, and organic material can develop in joints. Joint sand condition should also be considered.

Pollen & Organic Debris

Pollen, leaves, grass, and outdoor debris can settle across pavers and joints.

Surface Discoloration

Dark, green, or uneven discoloration may be associated with algae, mildew, dirt, or other buildup.

Weather-Related Buildup

Rain, humidity, sunlight, and repeated outdoor exposure can contribute to residue and discoloration.

THE RIGHT APPROACH

Why Paver Cleaning Requires Care

Paver Material & Condition Matter

Not every paver surface has the same material, age, condition, or installation quality. These factors should be considered before cleaning.

Joint Sand Requires Care

Pressure that is too aggressive can disturb joint sand. Cleaning should take the condition of the joints into consideration.

Avoid Excessive Pressure

More pressure does not automatically mean better cleaning. The method should fit the paver surface and existing buildup.

Identify Damaged Pavers

Loose, cracked, chipped, or damaged pavers should be identified before cleaning. Cleaning does not repair structural problems.

AFTER CLEANING

Paver Sealing After Cleaning

Cleaning and sealing are two different stages of paver maintenance. Pavers should be properly cleaned and prepared before a sealer is applied.

What Is Paver Sealing?

Paver sealing applies an appropriate protective coating or treatment to suitable paver surfaces. The sealer depends on material, condition, location, and desired finish.

When Should Pavers Be Sealed?

Timing depends on paver material, existing sealer, surface condition, exposure, and maintenance history. Sealing may be considered after proper preparation.

Preparing Pavers Before Sealing

Pavers should be clean and appropriately prepared. Visible dirt, buildup, weeds, and other concerns should be addressed first.

Why Proper Application Matters

Sealer application requires suitable coverage, preparation, and drying conditions. A dirty or damp surface can affect the final result.

BEFORE WE ARRIVE

What to Do Before Paver Cleaning & Sealing

  • Move tables, chairs, umbrellas, grills, and other furniture away from the paver area.
  • Clear toys, planters, decorations, tools, and other loose items from the area.
  • Keep nearby doors and windows closed while cleaning is underway.
  • Move or protect items that may be sensitive to water or cleaning treatments.
  • Mention loose pavers, cracked areas, damaged joints, existing sealer, drainage concerns, or other conditions.

PAVER CLEANING & SEALING FAQS

Common Questions

What is paver cleaning?

Paver cleaning removes visible dirt, grime, algae, mildew, pollen, organic debris, and other buildup from suitable paver surfaces.

Can you clean paver driveways?

Yes, suitable paver driveways can be cleaned according to their material, condition, and type of buildup.

Can you clean paver patios?

Yes, suitable paver patios can be cleaned to address visible dirt, algae, mildew, pollen, and organic buildup.

Can you clean paver joints?

Paver joints can be cleaned, but joint sand condition should be considered because excessive pressure can disturb it.

Will paver cleaning remove weeds?

Cleaning can address visible organic growth in joints, but recurring weeds may require additional maintenance or treatment.

What is paver sealing?

Paver sealing applies an appropriate sealer to prepared paver surfaces. The sealer depends on material, condition, location, and intended result.

Do pavers need to be cleaned before sealing?

Yes. Pavers should be properly cleaned and prepared so visible dirt, organic buildup, and other concerns are addressed first.

How often should pavers be sealed?

There is no universal schedule. Exposure, traffic, material, existing sealer, weather, and maintenance can affect resealing timing.

Can you seal pavers immediately after cleaning?

Sealing requires appropriate preparation and drying conditions. Pavers should be sufficiently clean and dry first.

Can damaged or loose pavers be repaired during cleaning?

Cleaning and repair are separate services. Damaged or loose pavers can be identified, but cleaning does not correct structural problems.

Will paver cleaning remove every stain?

No cleaning method can guarantee removal of every stain. Results depend on the type, age, depth, and condition of the stain.
